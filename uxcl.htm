Universal Experience Checklist






What are you missing without realizing it?


What are you missing without realizing it?

A list for checking whether the words we share are describing the same experiences. After Scott Alexander’s 2014 post of the same name, and Galton before it.

Every line below describes something most people report experiencing. Rate how well each one describes you, and mark the ones where you answered 1 or ?. Those are your candidates.

Answer literally. The question is whether the thing described actually happens to you, not whether it sounds like something people say. If you can’t tell whether a line describes a real experience or a figure of speech, that’s a ?, and it is the most useful mark on the page.

A few lines are worded as the absence of something — cilantro tasting of leaves rather than soap, a quiet room sounding quiet. That is deliberate. It keeps 1 meaning the same thing on every line, so there is only one pattern to look for.

Nothing here gets collected.

  • 1No, never
  • 2Faint or rare
  • 3Moderate, sometimes
  • 4Strong or frequent
  • 5Vivid, constant, unmistakable
  • ?I can’t tell what’s being asked

What the marks mean

  • A 1 means your answer sits away from the common report. It locates a difference, not a deficit, and roughly half of these have no better or worse side at all.
  • The useful exchange is describing what happens instead, concretely, to someone who answered 5. “Vivid” and “strong” are the words that let two different experiences pass for one, so push past them in both directions.
  • This list only finds absences. Someone whose memory or imagery is unusually strong will answer 5 throughout and get no flag, so a run of 5s is worth a second look even though it isn’t marked.
  • The best candidate is usually one nobody thought to write down. If you have an experience that no one around you seems to share, it belongs on the blank lines rather than on this list.
